Céragrès

REF Digital modernized Céragrès’s digital experience, making its extensive product catalog more intuitive, immersive and accessible for B2B and B2C audiences.

Responses by REF Digital.

Listen to audio version of this article

Background: For Céragrès, a Montréal-based manufacturer of architectural materials, the purpose of this new site was to radically modernize its digital presence, organize its extensive product catalog and create a seamless experience for its key audiences. We designed the platform to inspire everyday consumers while also meeting the high standards of architects, designer and major B2B stakeholders. With the catalog at the center of its experience, the site supports commercial growth and helps visitors transform their initial inspiration to a clearly defined project.

Design core: The site combines a premium visual identity with a strong focus on product discovery and usability. Its clean grid layout and fluid animations are inspired by the geometry of tiles and the movement of materials. Product textures, colors, sizes and technical specifications are presented in a clear, immersive way. The experience is supported by optimized navigation; intelligent filters; unified B2B and B2C project portfolios; and dynamic, indexable content designed to improve search visibility.

Favorite details: The product-page and sample-ordering experience is a particularly strong feature. Technical information is presented clearly: users can save their favorite products to a wishlist, and the selection can be shared with a showroom or converted into a sample order. The automated sample-ordering process creates a direct bridge between online discovery and the physical project, making it easier for users to validate materials and colors before moving forward.

Challenges: Building one digital ecosystem that could successfully serve two very different audiences while making a massive catalog easy to explore. The previous site was designed mainly for the general public, and it did not adequately reflect the scope of Céragrès’s commercial offerings. Therefore, the solution had to balance inspiration, technical depth, brand expression and efficient product discovery within a single coherent experience.

Navigation structure: We designed the navigation around discoverability. The inventory was structured by product type, while B2B and B2C projects were brought together within the same experience. Intelligent filters make it easier to find a specific product, texture or shade. The product journey naturally leads users from exploration to action through product details, wishlists, showroom appointments and sample orders. This structure makes a very broad offering feel intuitive, fast and manageable.

Personaal

The website of this Prague-based design firm visually represents its philosophy that the creative process is like a playground, a place with constant iteration and discovery.

Responses by Adam Zaňka, cofounder and designer, Personaal.

Background: We have been working together as a studio for about a year now and, as it usually goes, we thought it was probably good for a design studio to have a website. Primarily, we needed a functional place to present what we do and who we are. At the same time, we wanted the website to reflect something that is important to us: the design process itself can be a playground, a place to test things, make mistakes and fix them, and discover something along the way.

Favorite details: We enjoyed breaking the usual homepage structure and reducing it to a sphere gallery, without too much explanation or additional content around it. At the same time, there are smaller, sometimes hidden interactions throughout the site. They are not necessarily essential, but they might entertain you for a moment while browsing. We like when a functional website gives you an occasional reason to stop, play with something and then continue.

Challenges: Probably the fact that the client was us. When designing for yourself, it becomes very tempting to keep adding, changing and testing things forever. So one of the challenges was simply setting an internal deadline and accepting that at some point the website had to go live.

But that is also the magic of working in digital and online. A website does not really have to be finished—a year, a month or even tomorrow, it can become something completely different. We probably have a bit of a love–hate relationship with that.

Navigation structure: It came quite naturally from what the website primarily needs to do—present our work. Projects come first, followed by information about the studio, what we do and who we are. We wanted the basic structure to stay simple and functional, leaving more room for some fun within the homepage and the content itself.

Technology: The entire website was designed and built in Framer, with some parts custom coded.
